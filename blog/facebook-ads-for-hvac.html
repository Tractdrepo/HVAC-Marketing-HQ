Facebook ads for HVAC: when they work (seasonal tune-ups, retargeting)

Facebook isn't where homeowners search for a repair. It's where you remind them about a tune-up before the season and follow up with people who visited your site. How to use it for that.

By Logan, founder of HVAC Marketing HQ · Updated Oct 14, 2026

Key takeaways

  • Google catches people searching right now. Facebook reaches people before they need you.
  • The best HVAC uses are seasonal tune-ups, maintenance plans and retargeting site visitors.
  • The Meta Pixel on your site lets you measure results and build audiences of past visitors.
  • Target your real service area. Meta lets you set a radius around a city or a custom spot.
In this guide
  1. Search vs scroll
  2. When Facebook ads work for HVAC

Search vs scroll

On Google, a homeowner with a broken AC is looking for you. On Facebook and Instagram, they're scrolling past friends' photos. That's a different moment. Facebook ads rarely beat Google for "my furnace just died". Where they can work is reaching people before they need you, and staying in front of people who already looked at your site.

There's a big audience to reach. The EIA's 2020 home energy survey found 88% of U.S. households use air conditioning, with 92% in the Midwest and 93% in the South. Every one of those systems needs care.

When Facebook ads work for HVAC

  • Pre-season tune-ups. The BLS notes HVAC techs often work overtime in peak heating and cooling seasons. Ads for spring AC tune-ups and fall furnace checks fill the schedule before the rush, when your crew has room.
  • Maintenance plans. A plan is an easy offer to explain in a short ad, and it brings repeat work you own.
  • Retargeting. Show ads to people who visited your site but didn't call. They already know your name.
  • Past customers. Remind them it's time for service, or tell them about a new product like heat pumps.
  • Shoulder months. When search slows down, Facebook can keep the board from going empty.

When they usually don't

  • Emergency repair calls, where Google search and Local Services Ads are a better fit.
  • Brand-new shops with no reviews, photos or site to send people to.
  • Shops with no one to answer leads fast. A form lead that waits a day goes cold.

The setup that matters

  1. Install the Meta Pixel. Meta describes it as code on your website that tracks visitor activity, so you can measure how well ads work and build custom audiences for targeting.
  2. Build a website visitor audience. Meta's website custom audiences let you target people who visited your site within a time window you choose. Exclude people who already booked.
  3. Set your service area. Meta lets you target a radius around a city (10 to 50 miles) or around a custom location (about 0.63 to 50 miles). Match it to where your trucks actually go.
  4. Consider lead ads. Meta's lead ads use a mobile-friendly form that fills in details people already shared with Facebook, so it's quick to sign up for a tune-up. Then call fast.
  5. Send clicks to a matching page. A tune-up ad should land on a tune-up page with the price and a call button, not your homepage.

A simple seasonal plan

Before cooling season, run a spring AC tune-up offer to your service area and your past customers. Before heating season, switch to furnace and heat pump checks. All year, run a small retargeting ad to recent site visitors with your reviews and phone number. Judge it the same way as every other channel: cost per booked job, from your own records.

Questions

Do Facebook ads work for HVAC repair calls?

Usually not as well as Google, because people with a broken system search for help. Facebook works better for tune-ups, maintenance plans and retargeting.

What is retargeting?

Showing ads to people who already visited your website. The Meta Pixel on your site makes it possible.

Should I use Facebook lead ads?

They can work for tune-up and maintenance plan offers, as long as someone calls the lead back fast.
