HVAC website design: what a contractor site needs to book calls

A good HVAC website isn't about looks. It's about a homeowner on a phone finding your number, trusting you and calling in under a minute. Here's what that takes.

By Logan, founder of HVAC Marketing HQ · Updated Oct 14, 2026

Key takeaways

  • Design for a phone first. Your number and a call button should be on every screen.
  • One page per service and real town pages beat one long homepage.
  • Speed matters. Google's good mark for load time (LCP) is 2.5 seconds or less.
  • Track calls from the site, or you can't tell what it books.
In this guide
  1. Who you're designing for
  2. What every HVAC site needs
  3. Fast and mobile-friendly
  4. If you run ads, the site matters even more

Who you're designing for

Picture the customer: it's hot, the AC quit, and they're on a phone looking at three shops. They want to know three things fast. Do you do this? Do you come to my area? How do I reach you right now? A site that answers those in a few seconds books calls. A site that makes them hunt loses them to the next tab.

What every HVAC site needs

  1. A tap-to-call number at the top of every page. On a phone it should stay in view as they scroll.
  2. A clear headline. What you do and where: "AC and furnace repair in Waco and nearby towns." Not a slogan.
  3. One page per main service. AC repair, furnace repair, heat pumps, installs, maintenance plans. Google's SEO guide calls for helpful, people-first content, and a page about one thing matches a search for that thing.
  4. Real town pages. One page for each town you take calls in, with real jobs, photos and reviews from there. Google's spam policies call near-copy city pages that funnel to one page doorway abuse, so make each one real.
  5. Proof. Your real Google rating and reviews, photos of your crew and trucks, license numbers and years in business.
  6. A short form. Name, phone, what's wrong. Not 12 fields. Some people would rather type than call.
  7. Hours and emergency service, stated plainly. If you take after-hours calls, say so at the top.

For a full checklist, see the HVAC website checklist.

Fast and mobile-friendly

Google says Core Web Vitals are used by its ranking systems. It gives three marks for a good experience:

MeasureWhat it meansGoogle's good mark
Largest Contentful Paint (LCP)How fast the main content shows up2.5 seconds or less
Interaction to Next Paint (INP)How fast the page reacts when someone tapsUnder 200 milliseconds
Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS)How much the page jumps around while loadingUnder 0.1

Google also says good scores don't guarantee a top ranking, and it still shows the most relevant content first. So speed doesn't replace good pages. It keeps a homeowner from leaving before they see them. Big hero videos, sliders and chat pop-ups that cover the call button are the usual culprits.

Google also asks two simple questions in its page experience guide: does your content display well on mobile, and are your pages served securely (HTTPS)? Both should be a yes.

If you run ads, the site matters even more

Google Ads gives each keyword a Quality Score from 1 to 10, and landing page experience is one of its three parts: how relevant and useful your page is to the people who click your ad. Google's Ad Rank also weighs landing page quality. A matching service page can make the same click cost less. See Google Ads for HVAC.

Measure calls, not visits

Traffic doesn't pay the bills. Booked jobs do. Google Ads can count calls to a forwarding number shown on your site after an ad click, with a minimum call length you choose. Add call tracking for your other traffic too, so you can see which pages make the phone ring. And make sure every call gets answered, or texted back with missed-call text-back.

Questions

How many pages does an HVAC website need?

Enough to give each main service and each main town its own useful page, plus about, reviews and contact. For most shops that's more than five and fewer than you'd think.

Should I put prices on my website?

If you have a set price, like a tune-up or a diagnostic fee, showing it can help. For installs, explain what goes into the price and offer a free estimate.

Does a faster site rank higher?

Google says Core Web Vitals are used by its ranking systems, but relevant content still comes first. Speed mostly helps by keeping visitors from leaving.
