HVAC SEO: what actually moves rankings in the map and organic results

The map pack and the regular results rank differently. What Google says matters for each, what an HVAC company can change this month, and what to stop paying for.

By Logan, founder of HVAC Marketing HQ · Updated Oct 14, 2026

Key takeaways

  • The map pack runs on relevance, distance and prominence. You can't change distance.
  • Your Google profile's category, services and reviews do most of the map work.
  • Organic results run on helpful pages, clear titles and links from other sites.
  • Nobody can guarantee a #1 ranking. Google says so itself.
In this guide
  1. Two kinds of results, two sets of rules
  2. The map: relevance, distance, prominence
  3. Organic results: pages people find useful
  4. What to stop paying for
  5. How long it takes

Two kinds of results, two sets of rules

When a homeowner searches "AC repair" plus your town, Google usually shows ads, then a map with three businesses, then the regular website results. The map comes from your Google Business Profile. The regular results come from your website. They overlap, but they aren't the same thing, so they need different work.

The map: relevance, distance, prominence

Google says local results are based mainly on three things. Relevance is how well your profile matches the search. Distance is how far you are from the searcher. Prominence is how well known you are, including how many sites link to you and how many reviews you have. Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your local ranking.

What moves the map, in plain terms:

  1. The right primary category. Google says to pick the fewest categories that describe your core business, and to focus on the most specific ones.
  2. A real business name. Google says your name should match your real-world name, and extra words in it can get your profile suspended.
  3. A service area, done right. Google says service-area businesses should hide their address, keep one profile for the central office, and set a service area that generally stays within about 2 hours of driving.
  4. Steady reviews and replies. See how many Google reviews an HVAC company needs.
  5. Complete, current details. Google says complete and detailed information helps it match you to searches.

One thing doesn't move the map: money. Google says there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

Organic results: pages people find useful

Google's SEO Starter Guide says to make content helpful, reliable and people-first. For an HVAC site that means one clear page per service you want more of (AC repair, furnace repair, heat pumps, maintenance plans) that answers what a homeowner wants to know: do you serve my area, how fast can you come, what does it cost to start, and how do I book.

  • Clear title links. Google says the title link helps people decide which result to click. "AC Repair in Waco | Your Company" beats "Home".
  • Readable URLs. Google suggests words in the URL that help users, like /ac-repair.
  • Links from other sites. Google says it mainly finds pages through links from pages it already knows. Local suppliers, sponsorships and associations are good places to earn them.
  • A fast, mobile-friendly site. Google says Core Web Vitals are used by its ranking systems, but that good scores don't guarantee a top spot and that it still shows the most relevant content first.

What to stop paying for

  • Bought links. Google's spam policies list buying or selling links for ranking as link spam.
  • Copy-paste city pages. Google calls pages aimed at cities that all funnel to one page doorway abuse, and mass-made pages with little value scaled content abuse. A town page should have real work, real reviews and real details from that town.
  • Meta keywords. Google says it doesn't use the keywords meta tag.
  • Ranking guarantees. Google says no one can guarantee a #1 ranking, and that if an SEO promises one, you should look elsewhere.
  • Fake addresses. Google says P.O. boxes, remote mailboxes and virtual offices aren't eligible.

How long it takes

Google says some changes take effect in a few hours and others can take several months, and suggests waiting a few weeks before judging a change. Profile fixes are the quickest to make. New pages and links take longer to build.

Questions

Is the map pack the same as SEO?

They're related but different. The map comes from your Google Business Profile. The regular results come from your website. Good local SEO works on both.

Can I rank in a town where I don't have an office?

In the regular results, yes, with a real page about your work there. In the map, distance matters, and Google doesn't allow fake addresses to get around it.

Does running Google Ads help my SEO?

No. Google says you can't pay for a better local ranking. Ads show separately.
