HVAC marketing ideas that book jobs: 21 ideas sorted by cost

21 HVAC marketing ideas that book jobs, sorted into free, low cost and paid. Each one is short, practical and fair to your customers.

By Logan, founder of HVAC Marketing HQ · Updated Oct 15, 2026

Key takeaways

  • Start with the free ideas: your Google profile, reviews and past customers cost time, not money.
  • Google says you can't pay for a better map ranking, so the free work on your profile matters.
  • Ask every customer for a review. Google's rules ban picking only the happy ones.
  • Add paid ads once the phone is answered every time and you can track which calls book.
In this guide
  1. How to use this list
  2. Free ideas (cost: your time)
  3. Low cost ideas (cost: a tool or a small spend)
  4. Paid ideas (cost: an ad budget)
  5. What not to do
  6. Where to start this week

How to use this list

Most HVAC marketing lists are long and vague. This one is short and sorted by what it costs. The free ideas cost you time. The low cost ideas need a tool or a small spend. The paid ideas need an ad budget.

Do them roughly in order. Paid ads send more calls, but if calls go to voicemail or quotes never get a follow-up, more calls just means more waste. Fix the leaks first, then turn up the volume.

We left out cold calling and cold texting on purpose. Texas SB 140 took effect on September 1, 2025 and brings marketing texts under the state's telemarketing law. Market to people who asked to hear from you.

Free ideas (cost: your time)

  • 1. Fill in every part of your Google Business Profile. Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and that complete, detailed info helps you match searches. Set your category, services, hours and photos. See our Google Business Profile service.
  • 2. Set a service area instead of showing a home address. Google lets a business that travels to customers hide its address and show only the area it serves. Most HVAC shops fit that description.
  • 3. Post offers and updates on your profile. Google Business Profile has Updates, Offers and Events posts. Google archives posts older than 6 months unless you set a date range, so post a fresh tune-up offer each season.
  • 4. Ask every customer for a Google review. Your profile has a Get more reviews option that gives you a link or a QR code. Google's policy bans asking only happy customers, so ask everyone. Our review gap calculator shows how long it takes to catch the top shop.
  • 5. Reply to every review, good or bad. A short, calm reply shows the next homeowner that a real person runs the shop. Read our Google reviews guide for how to reply.
  • 6. Call back every missed call the same day. A missed call is often a job that went to the next shop. Run your own numbers in the missed call cost calculator.
  • 7. Remind past customers about tune-ups before the season. ENERGY STAR suggests a cooling check in spring and a heating check in fall, before contractors get busy. Email or call past customers who agreed to hear from you.
  • 8. Ask every caller how they found you. Write it on the job. After a month you'll know which channel books jobs and which just rings.

Low cost ideas (cost: a tool or a small spend)

  • 9. Text back missed calls automatically. When you can't pick up, the caller gets a text in about a minute and can reply with the problem. They called you first, so you're answering them, not cold texting. See missed-call text-back.
  • 10. Send a review request after every job. Automate the ask so it goes out to every customer, every time, right after the work is done. See review requests.
  • 11. Follow up on every open quote. A replacement quote that never gets a second touch is easy to lose. Use the estimate follow-up calculator to see what one extra close is worth, then set up estimate follow-up.
  • 12. Sell a maintenance plan. Plan members come back twice a year, and those visits often turn into repairs and replacements. Price yours with the maintenance plan calculator.
  • 13. Put your review QR code on invoices and leave-behinds. Google suggests places like receipts and thank you emails. Note that Google says the reviews QR code can only be made on a computer browser.
  • 14. Build a page for each service and each town. Google's own SEO guide says useful, people-first content is likely to matter more than any other tip in it. One clear page per service and town beats one page that lists everything. See HVAC websites and local SEO.
  • 15. Answer website visitors after hours. Chat can take the address and the problem at night so you can call back first thing. See AI web chat and the after-hours call calculator.
  • 16. Thank customers who send referrals. A thank-you card or a credit on their next tune-up for a referral is fine. Just never offer anything in exchange for a review, which Google bans.

Paid ideas (cost: an ad budget)

  • 17. Run Local Services Ads. Google charges per valid lead, not per click, and says leads it finds invalid aren't charged. Estimate a budget with the LSA cost calculator and see Local Services Ads.
  • 18. Run Google Ads with a call button. You pay per click. Google says a click on a call asset on mobile costs the same as a click on the headline. Plan spend with the Google Ads budget calculator and see Google Ads.
  • 19. Remind past website visitors on Facebook before the season. Meta's Pixel lets you build a custom audience of people who visited your site. A tune-up reminder to them works better than a cold ad. See Facebook ads.
  • 20. Plan spring and fall campaigns ahead of time. Line up your tune-up offer, profile post, email and ads before the first hot or cold week, not during it. See seasonal campaigns.
  • 21. Answer every call, even at peak. On the busiest days the phone rings while every tech and CSR is busy. An AI answering line can book or take a message so no caller hits voicemail. See AI phone answering.

What not to do

Some "ideas" you'll see online can cost you your profile or worse. Skip these:

  • Review gating. Sending only happy customers to Google, and the rest to a private form, is selective asking. Google's policy lists it as fake engagement and removes those reviews.
  • Paying for reviews. Google bans offering discounts or gifts for reviews. The FTC's rule on fake reviews, in effect since October 21, 2024, bans buying positive or negative reviews and fake reviews outright.
  • Cold texting and cold calling lists. In Texas, SB 140 puts marketing texts under the telemarketing law. Stick to people who reached out to you or agreed to hear from you.

Where to start this week

  1. Fix your Google profile (ideas 1 to 3).
  2. Start asking every customer for a review (ideas 4 and 10).
  3. Stop losing calls (ideas 6, 9 and 21).
  4. Then add one paid channel and track booked jobs from it (ideas 17 or 18).

Questions

What's the cheapest HVAC marketing that works?

Your Google Business Profile, reviews and past customers. They cost time, not ad money, and Google says you can't pay for a better map ranking anyway.

Can I text past customers about a tune-up?

Only people who agreed to hear from you. In Texas, SB 140 brings marketing texts under the state's telemarketing law, so don't text lists of people who never asked.

Can I ask only happy customers for reviews?

No. Google's policy bans selectively asking for positive reviews. Ask every customer, the same way, every time.

When should I start paid ads?

Once every call gets answered or called back and you can tell which calls became jobs. Otherwise you can't tell if the ads pay.
