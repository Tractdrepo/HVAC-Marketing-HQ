How to get more HVAC reviews on Google (within Google's rules)

A simple system for asking every customer for a Google review, what Google and the FTC don't allow, and how to reply so the next homeowner calls you.

By Logan, founder of HVAC Marketing HQ · Updated Oct 14, 2026

Key takeaways

  • Ask every customer, the same way, right after the job. A link or QR code makes it easy.
  • No discounts, gift cards or entries for reviews. Google prohibits it.
  • Don't filter out unhappy customers. Google bans selectively asking for positive reviews.
  • Reply to every review in your own words.
In this guide
  1. Why reviews matter for an HVAC shop
  2. The system: ask everyone, every time

Why reviews matter for an HVAC shop

Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking. Reviews also show up right next to your name in the map, so a homeowner with a dead furnace sees them before they call. More reviews, coming in steadily, help on both fronts.

The system: ask everyone, every time

Most shops don't have a review problem. They have an asking problem. Techs forget, the office is busy, and the request goes out in a burst once a quarter. A system fixes that:

  1. Get your review link. Google says you can ask customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code to leave a review. Put the QR code on invoices, leave-behind cards and the truck.
  2. Send it the same day. Ask while the visit is fresh, by text right after the job is closed. That's what review requests automate.
  3. Ask everyone. Every finished job gets the same request, whether the tech thinks it went great or not.
  4. Keep it simple. One short message, one link. Customers need to be signed into a Google account to leave a review, so don't bury the link.
  5. Follow up once. One reminder is fine. Pestering isn't.

What Google doesn't allow

Google's review policies are strict, and breaking them can get reviews removed. Here's what's out:

  • Incentives. Google prohibits offering free or discounted goods or services in exchange for posting a review, or for changing or removing a negative one. No "$25 off your next tune-up for a 5-star review".
  • Review gating. Google says businesses may not discourage negative reviews or selectively ask happy customers for reviews. That means no "rate us 1 to 5 first, and only 4s and 5s get the Google link" funnel.
  • Paid or fake reviews. Google removes reviews that are paid for, directly or in kind, or posted from many accounts by one person.
  • Pressure on site. Google says not to require or pressure customers to leave a review while you're at their home.
  • Review quotas for staff. Google lists asking staff to get a certain number of reviews as not allowed.
  • Scripted content. Don't ask customers to include specific words.

It isn't only Google. The FTC's 2024 rule bans buying or selling fake reviews, offering incentives tied to a positive or negative review, and using threats or intimidation to stop or remove a negative review.

Reply to every review

Google says replies are public and help you build relationships with customers, and advises against sending the same generic thank-you to everyone. Name the job if it's not private, thank them, and keep it short.

For a bad review, reply calmly, once. Say what you'll do to make it right and give a way to reach you. The next homeowner reads your reply more closely than the complaint.

How many is enough?

Google doesn't publish a target. Compare yourself with the three shops in the map for your main service and town. See how many Google reviews an HVAC company needs for more on that.

Questions

Can I give a discount for leaving a review?

No. Google prohibits offering free or discounted goods or services in exchange for reviews, and the FTC rule bans incentives tied to a review's sentiment.

Can I only send the review link to happy customers?

No. Google says businesses may not selectively ask for positive reviews. Ask every customer the same way.

Can I ask a customer to delete a bad review?

You can't offer anything in exchange for removing it, and you can't threaten them. Reply publicly, try to fix the problem, and let the customer decide.
