Google Local Services Ads for HVAC: how they work and how leads are charged

What Local Services Ads are, what counts as a charged lead, what Google says affects your ranking, and how credits work when a lead isn't real.

By Logan, founder of HVAC Marketing HQ · Updated Oct 14, 2026

Key takeaways

  • Local Services Ads charge per lead (a call, message or booking), not per click.
  • Google says missed calls and slow replies can hurt your ranking.
  • Reviews, response time, photos and completed verification all count toward your profile quality.
  • Invalid or low-quality leads aren't charged, and Google may credit them back later.
In this guide
  1. What Local Services Ads are
  2. How you're charged
  3. What Google says affects your ranking
  4. When a lead isn't a real lead
  5. Getting more out of it

What Local Services Ads are

Local Services Ads are the listings with the Google Verified badge that show at the top of Google for searches like "furnace repair near me". Google calls them pay-per-lead listings for verified local service providers. A homeowner sees your name, rating and hours, and can call, message or book from the ad.

To get the badge, your business goes through Google's verification checks. Google says it will place a listing below providers who've finished all onboarding requirements until you're done.

How you're charged

With regular Google Ads you pay for clicks. With Local Services Ads you pay for leads. Google says you're charged for each valid lead you get through the ad, and your monthly budget caps what you spend.

Here's what Google says counts:

Lead typeWhen it's charged
Phone callWhen you answer, or reach the customer. For a missed call, the customer has to stay on the line more than 20 seconds.
MessageWhen you get a text or email from the customer. Google says message leads are usually priced lower than calls, though not always.
BookingBooking requests are charged in the US and Canada only, where bookings are offered.
Repeat contactFollow-up calls or messages from the same customer within 15 days aren't charged again.

Google says lead prices vary by location, job type, lead type and bidding mode. That's why we don't quote a typical cost per lead. Your account shows what you're actually paying.

What Google says affects your ranking

Google lists the factors in its Local Services help pages:

  • Your bid. The most you're willing to pay for a lead. You may pay less.
  • How likely you are to get the lead. This includes how fast you respond, the service and location searched, the services in your profile, and whether you accept messages and bookings.
  • Profile quality. Ratings, review count, average response time, good photos and completed verification.
  • A healthy mix of providers. Google says it also tries to give all providers a fair chance.

Google is blunt about one thing: if you regularly fail to answer calls or reply to messages, your ad ranking may be affected. For an HVAC shop on a roof all afternoon, that's the real risk. Missed-call text-back and fast replies protect it.

Google also recommends its Maximize Leads bid mode, where it sets your bid to get the most leads within your budget.

When a lead isn't a real lead

Not every call is a job. Some are wrong numbers, sales pitches or jobs you don't do. Google says leads it finds invalid or low quality aren't charged. It also says charged leads are reassessed over time and may be credited automatically if later found to be low quality, and that in most cases credits land within 30 days.

You can also rate each lead in your account and dispute one you think isn't valid. A successful dispute is credited back in the US and Canada. Make it a habit: whoever handles leads checks them every week.

Getting more out of it

  1. List only the services and areas you actually want. A lead for a job you won't take still costs money.
  2. Turn on messages and bookings if your team can answer them fast.
  3. Keep asking for reviews. Rating and review count are part of profile quality. See review requests.
  4. Check leads weekly, rate them and dispute the bad ones.

Questions

Do I pay for clicks on Local Services Ads?

No. Google charges for valid leads, such as calls, messages and bookings, not for clicks or views.

Am I charged for missed calls?

Google says a missed call can be charged if the customer stays on the line more than 20 seconds. That's why answering fast matters.

Do Local Services Ads help my map ranking?

No. They show separately, at the top of the page. Google says you can't pay for a better local ranking.
