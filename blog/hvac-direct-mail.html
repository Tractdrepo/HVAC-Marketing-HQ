HVAC direct mail: what to send, who to send it to, and how to track it

Direct mail for HVAC companies: the campaigns worth running, how to pick the homes, handwritten letters vs postcards, the offer, and how to track every job.

By Logan, founder of HVAC Marketing HQ · Updated Oct 25, 2026

Key takeaways

  • Mail works best when it has a trigger: a finished install, a season turning, a quote going quiet.
  • Pick the homes on purpose. Owner-occupied homes in your service area, built in the right years, beat a whole mail route.
  • Handwritten letters get opened like personal mail. Postcards cost less per home for big lists.
  • Give homeowners a real reason to call now, and track every piece with its own link, QR code and claim code.

Why mail still works for HVAC

Homeowners see ads all day, on their phones, their TVs and every site they visit. Most of it blurs together. A piece of mail in their hand, especially a handwritten one with their name on it, is one of the few things that still gets their full attention for a moment.

Handwritten mail companies report open rates of 99% or more for handwritten notes. That figure comes from the vendors themselves, not an independent study, but the reason behind it is easy to see: a handwritten envelope looks like a letter from a person, so it gets opened instead of sorted into the recycling.

Mail won't replace Google. When the AC quits, people search. Mail's job is different: being the name they already know when that day comes, and reaching the homes that are likely to need you before they start searching.

Start with the ones that run on their own. Install neighbors goes out every time you finish a job. Tune-up season fills the slow weeks before summer and winter. Add the rest once those are tracked.

Pick the homes on purpose

The cheapest way to waste a stamp is mailing a home that will never call you. A few filters fix most of it:

  • Owner-occupied. Renters rarely buy a system. In Texas, county appraisal records show which homes have a homestead exemption, which means the owner lives there.
  • Year built. The same appraisal records list when each home was built. A street of homes from the same years often has systems coming due at the same time.
  • Your service area. Only homes your trucks can reach on a normal day.
  • Not your current customers, unless the campaign is for them, and never anyone who asked not to be mailed.

Every Door Direct Mail (EDDM) from USPS is the other option. You mail every home on a route without a list, addressed to "Postal Customer". It's cheaper per piece, but it reaches renters and new builds alike. Our HVAC advertising guide covers how EDDM works.

Postage matters for small batches. The USPS Forever stamp went to 82 cents on July 12, 2026. Marketing Mail prices only apply to mailings of 200 or more addressed pieces, so a 25-home neighbor mailing goes First-Class.

Give homeowners a reason to call now

"10% off" doesn't make anyone pick up the phone this month. A good homeowner offer has three parts:

  1. A reason for the deal. "We're already working on your street" makes a lower price believable.
  2. A real deadline. A date a few weeks out gets people to act now instead of someday.
  3. Something for later. Most homes won't need you this month. A credit on a new system that lasts a year keeps your mail in the drawer until they do.

Only quote a "regular" price you really charge. The FTC's guide on former price comparisons says the earlier price has to be one you offered in good faith for a reasonable time.

Track every piece

If you can't tell which mailing made the phone ring, you can't tell whether to send another. Put four things on every campaign:

  1. A tracked web address. Google Analytics reads utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign tags on a link, so visits from each mailing are counted separately.
  2. A QR code that points to that tracked link and a page built for the offer.
  3. A claim code on each piece, tied to the address it was mailed to.
  4. A check against your jobs. At 60, 90 and 180 days, match the mailed addresses to your booked jobs. People who just called your main line still count.

Then judge the mail on booked jobs and dollars, not calls. Our cost per lead calculator shows what each booked job cost by channel.

Questions

Does direct mail work for HVAC companies?

It works best with a trigger and a target: neighbors of a finished install, past customers before tune-up season, or homes with aging systems. Track every piece so you can tell.

Do handwritten letters really get opened?

Handwritten mail companies report open rates of 99% or more. It is a vendor figure, but a handwritten envelope looks like personal mail, so people open it.

How many homes should I mail after an install?

Many shops mail the nearest 25 to 50 homes. Fewer than 200 pieces means First-Class postage, not Marketing Mail prices.

How do I know which jobs came from my mail?

Use a tracked link, a QR code and a claim code on every piece, then match the mailed addresses against your booked jobs at 60, 90 and 180 days.
